Why this phase existed

Situation → Complication → Question. The answer is the rest of this chronicle.

Situation

v3.1 had already shipped the chronicle schema (P113), the context-pack builder (P114) and the HTML renderer (P115). SGSD could produce a phase chronicle.

Complication

Nothing yet checked that a chronicle was true. An ungrounded chronicle — one that asserts things no evidence backs — would quietly become a fog machine instead of a clarity tool.

Question

How does SGSD guarantee a chronicle is grounded in real evidence before it ever reaches the operator — and refuse to close the phase if it isn't?

03

What changed, in plain terms

No internal jargon used below without a plain-English expansion on first mention.

The short version

P116 built a validator — a small program that reads a finished chronicle and re-checks every claim against the original evidence. If a claim has no evidence behind it, the validator says REPORT_UNGROUNDED and the phase cannot close. That is what "binding gate" means: it's not advisory, it stops the line.

Crucially, the validator re-derives its answer from the raw evidence — it never trusts the chronicle's own summary of itself.

What landed on disk

11 files: the validator itself (validate-chronicle.cjs), a shell wrapper for hooking it into phase-close, and 8 benchmark fixtures — 4 examples of a good chronicle and 4 of a broken one — so the validator's own behaviour is locked by tests.

The self-test grew to 56 checks, all green — 23 from earlier phases, 16 new for P116, plus the running total.

Evidence — files changed (execution_receipt CMBs)
